Hazards found

HZ-016 · A token that calls the recipient back mid-transfer drains a counterparty that settles after paying

class 6 asset-handling confirmed on this target

This token is an ERC777: transferring to a contract calls that contract back before the transfer returns, resolved through the ERC1820 registry, and the ERC777 send() additionally requires a contract recipient to have registered an implementer. A counterparty that credits on deposit and only clears the credit after paying out can therefore be re-entered by the recipient, paid twice out of one balance, and left unable to pay the next honest depositor. The token is not at fault and is behaving to spec. imBTC was the token in the Lendf.Me drain of 2020-04-19, roughly 25 million dollars in one re-entrancy against exactly this shape, which is why a token whose selector set says ERC777 is worth knowing about before it is accepted, not after.

What is at stake: up to the credited balance a naive counterparty is holding; about 25M USD in one event against imBTC in 2020.

What to do: Not a token bug: any contract that accepts this token must clear its credit before paying out, or re-enter-proof its payout path. Where the counterparty cannot be changed, deposit through a wrapper that uses transfer() and keeps the callback off the hot path.
